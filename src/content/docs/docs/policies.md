---
title: Policies & Guidelines
description: Aurora Silicon’s existing provenance, disclosure, and contribution policies.
---

These apply everywhere Aurora Silicon operates: our Discord, our repositories,
and anywhere else the project's work is discussed. They are deliberately short,
and they are subject to change.

Nearly all of it reduces to one idea: do not bring material into this project
that you do not have the right to bring, and do not carry our work somewhere it
should not go. For the mechanics of actually submitting a change, see
[Contributing](/docs/contributing/).

## Follow the law

Do not break the law. Most of the rules below are essentially this, particularly
where commercial, proprietary, or confidential material is concerned.

## Apple and Microsoft employees and contractors

If you work for Apple or Microsoft, including as a contractor or subcontractor
for either company, do not contribute code to this project or to any of our
repositories unless both of the following are true:

- You have been explicitly authorised to do so by your employer, and
- An Aurora Silicon administrator has reviewed satisfactory proof of that
  authorisation.

:::caution[If in doubt, do not contribute]
This is not a formality. If you are unsure whether your employment or
contract permits you to contribute, the answer is to hold off and ask an
administrator first.
:::

## Confidential and non-public material

Do not share NDA-protected or otherwise non-public software, code,
documentation, reverse-engineering work, or other confidential material. Public
betas and publicly available developer betas are excluded from this restriction.

Any contribution resulting from unreleased, confidential, or otherwise
non-public code, software, documentation, hardware, or products will result in
the immediate removal of the content, and may result in the accounts involved
being banned. Depending on the nature and severity of the material provided, we
may also disclose the incident to the appropriate parties.

## LLMs and generative AI

If you use an LLM (ChatGPT, Claude, GLM or any other), you remain solely
responsible for everything you produce and submit with it. The tool does not
carry the responsibility; you do.

Make sure your agent follows the `AGENTS.md` instructions carried throughout our
repositories. Those instructions are what keep our requirements around code
provenance, licensing, and repository cleanliness intact, and an agent that
ignores them will produce work we cannot accept.

Every rule on this page applies unchanged when an LLM is involved. Using
open-weight, uncensored, "abliterated", or otherwise modified models exempts you
from none of it.

### Disclosing AI-generated contributions

Any submission containing content partially or fully generated by an LLM or
other generative AI must clearly disclose this in the associated commit. The
disclosure must be clear enough that maintainers and future contributors can
tell AI was involved in producing the submitted content.

This applies regardless of how substantially the generated output was
subsequently reviewed, edited, rewritten, or incorporated. Failure to disclose
AI involvement may result in the contribution being rejected or removed and,
where appropriate, further action being taken by the project administrators.

If an AI generated it under your direction, treat it as your contribution and
your responsibility, and disclose it.

## Projects we do not send work to

Do not report bugs, open issues or pull requests, make suggestions, or send
project-derived findings to Asahi Linux, NT-for-ASi, Mesa, FEX, or any project
that does not accept AI-assisted work, or to their maintainers.

Our approach to LLM usage and decompilation differs from theirs, and they treat
anything produced by Aurora Silicon, or as a result of work done within this
project, as unusable. Keep our development, findings, patches,
reverse-engineering work and related technical discussion out of their spaces.

:::caution[The upstream Linux kernel is fine]
Contributing to the upstream Linux kernel is fine, under its own rules and the
rest of this page.
:::

This is not about credit or secrecy. We depend heavily on those projects, and
the rule protects their work from contamination by ours. It is not there to
keep our work to ourselves.

## If you are unsure

Ask an administrator before you act, not after. Every rule here is easier to
follow in advance than to unwind later: content that should not have been shared
cannot be un-shared, and a contribution built on material we cannot accept has
to be removed no matter how good it is.
